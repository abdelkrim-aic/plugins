# NDA analysis

Apply the seven company rules in [references/gate-rules.md](references/gate-rules.md) to each NDA received for review. Read that reference before assessing the agreement. Review from GeoPost's perspective unless the user identifies another represented party; do not silently assume another company has adopted GeoPost's policy.

## Review

1. Read the full NDA, relevant schedules and amendments. Record the document/version, represented entity, counterparty, business purpose, direction of information sharing, governing law, dispute forum, agreement term, and confidentiality survival period. Use available deal context. Ask only for material missing facts, while continuing checks that do not depend on them.
2. Assess all seven rules, including omissions and conflicting provisions. Cite each relevant clause/section and page when available; otherwise use a heading and short exact quotation. Never invent clause numbers, text, or page references. Distinguish an absent clause from text that could not be accessed. An incomplete extract cannot support an unconditional GO.
3. Read the effect of each clause, not just keywords. An exclusion of consequential loss is different from accepting it. Inspect indemnities, carve-outs, survival language and cross-references that could undo an otherwise acceptable clause.
4. Distinguish strategic requirements, legal issues and commercial discretion. Do not convert preferences into absolute prohibitions. Record any user-supplied exception and its scope; do not treat it as a permanent change to the worksheet.
5. Give a decision and usable edits in the user's language; draft replacement clauses in the NDA's language. Preserve defined terms and fix affected cross-references. Label any newly drafted wording as a proposal, rather than claiming it is an approved template.

## Decision logic

The following decision labels operationalize the worksheet for the user's requested go/no-go output; they are not an approval procedure stated in the workbook.

- **GO:** All seven checks are sufficiently evidenced and aligned, with any commercial departure expressly accepted by the project owner in the available context. State any residual observations. GO is a worksheet-based recommendation, not a signature or a guarantee of enforceability.
- **NO-GO as drafted:** A confirmed strategic breach, the prohibited combination of non-EU governing law and non-EU courts, a material commercial mismatch, or a required commercial decision remains unresolved. State exactly what must change or be decided. This means hold signature pending resolution, not abandon negotiations.
- **HOLD — insufficient information / legal review:** No confirmed blocker determines the result, but missing text, unclear information flows, missing business context, or an unresolved legal issue prevents a reliable go/no-go. Identify the precise missing evidence or legal question. If a confirmed blocker also exists, lead with NO-GO and list the uncertainties separately.

Never average away a blocker with favorable checks or invent numerical risk scores. Use **High** for confirmed strategic/prohibited legal exposure, **Medium** for material commercial departures or unresolved legal interpretation, **Low** for minor drafting clarity, and **Unknown** when evidence is insufficient. Explain the impact; these severity labels are an analytical convention, not workbook ratings.

## Deliverable

Lead with the recommendation, whether signature can proceed under this review, and the main reasons. Include:

- A seven-row review table: **Check | Worksheet category | Status (Pass / Change needed / Unclear) | NDA evidence | Risk and practical impact | Required action**. Include severity with each issue.
- Prioritized edits: **clause/location, current wording or omission, delete/replace/insert action, proposed wording, rationale, and any unresolved choice**. For penalties, propose deletion rather than negotiating a lower amount. For a missing clause, say “insert” rather than fabricating original text.
- A short list of outstanding business facts, project-owner choices, or legal questions, with a proposed next action. Do not invent approvers or imply anyone approved the document.

For revised NDAs, reassess all seven points and report which prior issues are resolved, remain, or were introduced. Recheck interactions between changes before upgrading to GO.

## Scope and source handling

Use the bundled rules as the captured company baseline, citing its source/version. If the user provides updated rules, compare and apply the update explicitly. Do not silently replace company rules with general market practice. Supplemental issues may be reported separately as “outside the seven-point worksheet”; do not present them as additional company prohibitions.

The worksheet contains broad statements about courts, French law and arbitration. Treat them as source commentary, not verified current law. A policy comparison alone does not require legal research; when making a jurisdiction-specific legal or enforceability claim, verify current authoritative sources and distinguish that legal analysis from the company policy. Surface a focused legal-review question when it cannot be resolved.

Default to an inline report and proposed edits. Produce an annotated or redlined file when requested and supported; do not claim a file was highlighted or amended unless it was actually edited. Review authorization does not authorize signing, sending to counterparties, changing the source workbook, or monitoring an inbox.
